# General EU Immigration Concepts (Internal Reference)

## Who needs a visa?
- **EU/EEA/Switzerland citizens**: Freedom of movement within the EU; register locally if staying long-term in another EU state
- **Non-EU nationals**: Usually need a visa for entry and a residence permit for stays over 90 days (Schengen rules apply to Schengen area countries)

## Schengen area
- 90/180 day rule for short stays with a Schengen visa or visa-free entry (where applicable)
- Not all European countries are in Schengen (e.g. Ireland is EU but not Schengen)

## Common document categories
1. Valid passport (often 6+ months validity beyond planned stay)
2. Completed visa/residence application forms
3. Passport photos meeting country specifications
4. Proof of accommodation
5. Proof of financial means
6. Health/travel insurance
7. Criminal background check (for long-stay)
8. Employment contract or university admission (route-dependent)
9. Birth/marriage certificates (family routes; may need apostille/translation)

## Typical process flow
1. Determine correct visa/residence category for your purpose (work, study, family, etc.)
2. Gather documents per official checklist
3. Apply at embassy/consulate or online portal (country-specific)
4. Enter country if visa approved
5. Register address locally
6. Apply for residence permit / national ID card
7. Renew permit before expiry; track path to permanent residence or citizenship if desired

## Integration requirements
Many countries require:
- Language courses (A2–B1 level common for permanent residence)
- Civic integration exams
- Proof of stable income and housing

## Important reminders
- Never overstay a visa — serious consequences for future applications
- Use only official government websites for forms and fees
- Certified translations may be required for non-local language documents
- This is general guidance, not legal advice
