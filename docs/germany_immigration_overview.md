# Germany — Immigration Overview (Internal Reference)

## Visa types (non-EU nationals)

### Schengen short-stay visa (Type C)
- For stays up to 90 days in 180 days (tourism, business, family visit)
- Apply at German embassy/consulate in country of residence
- Typical documents: valid passport, application form, photos, travel insurance, proof of accommodation, financial means, itinerary

### National visa (Type D) — long stay
- Required for stays over 90 days (work, study, family reunion, job seeker)
- Apply before entering Germany; convert to residence permit after arrival
- Categories include: work visa, EU Blue Card, student visa, family reunion, freelance/self-employed

### EU Blue Card
- For highly qualified employment
- Generally requires: university degree (or comparable qualification), employment contract with minimum salary threshold (updated annually — verify current figure on official sites)
- Leads to permanent settlement options after qualifying period

## Residence permit (Aufenthaltstitel)
- Issued by local Ausländerbehörde (foreigners' office) after arrival with D visa
- Required documents often include: passport, visa, registration certificate (Anmeldung), proof of health insurance, employment contract or enrollment letter, proof of accommodation, biometric photos, fees

## Registration (Anmeldung)
- Register address at Bürgeramt within 14 days of moving in
- Bring passport, rental contract (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung), and completed registration form

## Work permit
- EU/EEA/Swiss citizens: no work permit required
- Non-EU: work authorization usually tied to residence permit type (e.g. employment visa, Blue Card, skilled worker route)

## Integration
- Integration courses (language + orientation) may be mandatory or recommended
- B1 German often required for permanent residence and citizenship pathways
